# Lost in Translation

Subtitle

Translating Technical Work into Shared Understanding

---

# Narrative

This presentation teaches engineers how to better merchandise technical work.

Merchandising is not marketing.

Merchandising is the deliberate practice of translating technical work into
language that creates understanding, alignment, and informed decisions.

The audience should leave with practical tools they can immediately use to:

- raise ideas earlier
- explain technical work more clearly
- advocate for themselves and others
- make better requests
- connect technical work to customer, operational and business value

Clarify the Signal

Purpose

Teach the questions that tighten an observed signal before translation.

What outcome are we trying to achieve?

Who needs to understand it?

# Commander Acorn

Commander Acorn represents the engineer.

He is competent.

Curious.

Professional.

He never solves the audience's problems.

He helps them notice.

He models thoughtful communication.

He is not the hero.

The audience is.

---

# Narrative Rules

Never introduce a new framework.

Never drift into leadership philosophy.

Never turn the presentation into career advice.

Never make merchandising synonymous with persuasion.

Always return to shared understanding.

---

# Canonical Message

Technical excellence deserves equal excellence in communication.

Merchandising is how we translate technical work into shared understanding.

Shared understanding creates better decisions.

Better decisions create momentum.

That is the mission.
